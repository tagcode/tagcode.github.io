    Uniform.Matrix.Calls

    Matrix 1:1 voice and video calls for the client engine, of two kinds:

    • RtcCall: MatrixRTC through a LiveKit SFU, as Element X and Element Call make calls, over Uniform.LiveKit.
    • LegacyCall: the m.call.* events of version 1, directly between the apps, as Element on the desktop and Element Classic make calls.

    Both are a VoiceCall, which the apps show the same way. Media goes over Uniform.Matrix.WebRtc, voice through Uniform.Matrix.Audio, and video is decoded with Uniform.Matrix.Video.

    Service

    CallService is a singleton beside the client engine. It follows SyncProcessor.Applied, routes each call event to its call, and raises IncomingCall for a fresh invite or ring to this user. It places MatrixRTC calls when the homeserver has a LiveKit transport, unless UseMatrixRtc is off, and legacy calls otherwise. Events go out through the OutboxSender, which encrypts them in encrypted rooms, as Element does. TURN credentials come from the homeserver's /voip/turnServer, and are kept until shortly before they expire.

    To add calls to an app, register the service, and set the devices when the app chooses them:

    builder.Services.AddSingleton<CallService>();
// …
calls.Devices = () => AudioDevices.ForThisPlatform(microphone, speaker);
calls.IncomingCall += call => ShowRinging(call);
VoiceCall call = await calls.PlaceCallAsync(roomId);

    A MatrixRTC call

    1. Joining. An OpenID token buys a LiveKit token from the homeserver's authorization service at /sfu/get, so this side's identity on the SFU is @user:server:DEVICE. The call publishes on the SFU and writes its membership, the state event org.matrix.msc3401.call.member with state key _@user:server_DEVICE_m.call. A delayed event of empty content (MSC4140) ends the membership 18 seconds after the device stops restarting it, every 4 seconds.
    2. Ringing. The caller sends org.matrix.msc4075.rtc.notification with ring, referring to its membership. The callee rings while the caller is in the call, until it answers by joining, or declines with org.matrix.msc4310.rtc.decline.
    3. Connected. Once the peer's membership is there, each side sends its media key in io.element.call.encryption_keys to the peer's call devices over Olm, through CryptoService.SendEncryptedToDevicesAsync. Keys come back in ToDeviceReceived. In encrypted rooms, frames are encrypted as Element Call encrypts them. A peer on another homeserver is subscribed to on its own SFU, with a token of its service's /get_token.
    4. Reconnecting. After a network change, a lost signalling connection, or a media path down for 2 seconds, the call joins its SFU again with a new token. It publishes again and moves the media there, and the others' SFUs are joined again. The membership, the keys and the identity on the SFU stay, so the peer goes on decrypting. The call ends after 45 seconds without success. When the delayed leave was sent meanwhile, the membership is written again. LiveKit does not pass on the video orientation, so the camera is told to make its pictures Upright before encoding. When the homeserver's authorization service takes delayed leaves, as its delegate_delayed_leave tells, the call hands its leave over with a delay of an hour and restarts it itself only every 5 minutes, as Element Call does. The service sends it when this side drops off LiveKit. When the handover fails, the call keeps a short leave of its own. After reconnecting, a leave the service sent meanwhile is armed again, and the membership written again.
    5. Ended. Hanging up sends the delayed leave at once and leaves the SFUs. The call ends when the peer's membership goes, or on a decline or a timeout.

    A legacy call

    A LegacyCall:

    1. Ringing or inviting. The caller sends m.call.invite with the offer, and the callee rings until it answers, rejects, or the invite's lifetime passes.
    2. Connecting. The callee answers with m.call.answer; the caller picks the first answer with m.call.select_answer, so the user's other devices stop ringing. Candidates go both ways in m.call.candidates batches, ending with an empty candidate; those that arrive before the offer or answer wait for it.
    3. Connected. VoiceMedia sends the microphone as Opus, and plays what arrives through a jitter buffer, paced by the speaker. Muted stops sending. When the caller sends video, IsVideo is set while it rings, and the answer accepts the video, unless CallService.ReceiveVideo is off. VideoReceiver decodes it on its own task and the pictures come in PictureReceived. When decoding falls behind or a frame does not decode, frames are skipped up to a keyframe, which the peer is asked for. AnswerWithVideoAsync also sends this side's camera, from the platform's IVideoCamera in CallService.Camera: its encoded VP8 frames go to the peer once connected, and the peer's keyframe requests go to its encoder. PlaceVideoCallAsync calls with video. The camera's rotation goes with its frames, and its small Previewed pictures come in PreviewReceived; CameraOff pauses it, and SwitchCamera changes cameras. The peer's video is decoded by the platform's VideoDecoder, e.g. Android's hardware one, or by the C# decoder; a device that cannot keep up asks the sender for less, and for full quality again once it can.
    4. Ended. m.call.hangup from either side, m.call.reject, a timeout, or a failed connection; EndReason says which.

    Tests

    Uniform.Chat.Server.Tests calls over MatrixRTC between two client engines of a homeserver with LiveKit on, when LIVEKIT_SERVER names LiveKit's server program. It checks the ring, the answer, encrypted voice both ways, and a hangup that ends the call on both sides.

    Uniform.Matrix.Calls.Tests runs two legacy calls against each other in memory, with fake devices that record and play tones in real time: a call that each side must hear and then hang up, and a rejected call. Where Chromium is installed, it calls with a canvas as its camera, and the call must decode its pictures.

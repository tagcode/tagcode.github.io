    Uniform.Matrix.WebRtc

    The subset of WebRTC that a 1:1 call needs, in C#: one Opus audio stream each way, and VP8 video each way, interoperating with browsers, Element and LiveKit's SFU. Only DTLS uses a library, BouncyCastle; the rest is written here against the RFCs and their test vectors. Nothing here knows about Matrix; Uniform.Matrix.Calls builds calls on it.

    Layers

    STUN and TURN come from Uniform.Stun: StunClient transactions and the TurnClient, on the messages of Uniform.Stun.Protocol.

    Folder What Specification
    Ice/ IceAgent: host, server-reflexive and relayed candidates, connectivity checks, role conflicts, nomination, peer-reflexive candidates, consent; IceCandidate as SDP writes it RFC 8445, RFC 8839, RFC 7675
    Dtls/ DtlsSrtp: DTLS 1.2 with BouncyCastle, use_srtp, the peer's certificate checked against its fingerprint, keys exported; DtlsCertificate self-signed ECDSA RFC 5764
    Srtp/ SrtpSession: SRTP and SRTCP with AES_CM_128_HMAC_SHA1_80, rollover counters, replay windows RFC 3711
    Rtp/ RtpPacket and JitterBuffer; VP8 frames put together by Vp8Depacketizer and split by Vp8Packetizer; receiver reports and picture loss indications of Rtcp, counted by ReceiverStatistics RFC 3550, RFC 7741, RFC 4585
    Sdp/ SessionDescription, and AudioSdp offers and answers: bundled, RTCP multiplexed, other media rejected RFC 8866, RFC 8829

    One UDP socket carries everything. Its datagrams are told apart by their first byte, as RFC 7983 says: STUN 0–3, DTLS 20–63, TURN channel data 64–79, and RTP and RTCP 128–191.

    A connection

    AudioPeerConnection puts the layers together. The caller makes an offer, the callee answers, both trickle their candidates to the other, and both connect. The offerer controls ICE, and the answerer is the DTLS client, as browsers do.

    The following shows both sides in one process; in a call, the strings travel in Matrix events:

    await using AudioPeerConnection caller = new(), callee = new();
caller.CandidateGathered += candidate => callee.AddRemoteCandidate(candidate.ToString());
callee.CandidateGathered += candidate => caller.AddRemoteCandidate(candidate.ToString());
callee.AudioReceived += packet => Play(decoder.Decode(packet.Payload));

string answer = callee.AcceptOffer(caller.CreateOffer());
caller.AcceptAnswer(answer);
await Task.WhenAll(caller.ConnectAsync(), callee.ConnectAsync());
await caller.SendAudioAsync(encoder.Encode(frame));

    The voice and video pipelines see a connection as an IMediaConnection, so they also run over the connections of an SFU call together, such as LiveKitMedia.

    TURN servers, e.g. those of a homeserver's /voip/turnServer, go in PeerConnectionOptions.Servers; RelayOnly keeps the call on the relay, so the peer does not learn this device's addresses.

    Received video

    With PeerConnectionOptions.ReceiveVideo, an answer accepts the VP8 video of the peer's offer as recvonly, bundled with the audio; ReceivesVideo tells whether it did. The packets are put together into whole frames, given in order in VideoReceived, for a decoder such as that of Uniform.Matrix.Video.

    A frame waits for packets that come out of order. After a packet is lost, only a keyframe is given, and the connection asks the peer for one with an RTCP picture loss indication; RequestKeyframeAsync asks for one too, e.g. when a frame did not decode. Receiver reports go to the peer every second, so it can see the loss and the round trip.

    Sent video

    With PeerConnectionOptions.SendVideo, an answer also sends video when the peer's offer receives it; the section names this side's video SSRC and stream, so browsers make a track of it. SendVideoAsync takes encoded VP8 frames, e.g. from a hardware encoder, and sends each in RTP packets of at most 1100 bytes, timed by a 90 kHz clock from the moment of sending. When the peer asks for a keyframe, with a picture loss indication or a full intra request, KeyframeRequested tells the encoder.

    A call's caller offers video with CreateOffer(video: true): both ways, and the answer decides which ways it goes.

    SFUs

    An SFU, such as LiveKit, needs a few more things of a connection:

    • Publishing: with SendOnly, an offer only sends, and AudioTrackId and VideoTrackId name the tracks in their msid, as the SFU was told of them.
    • Subscribing: the SFU's offer may carry no audio before anyone publishes, and a data channel section, which is rejected. The transport connects all the same, its credentials at the session level when every section is rejected. Later offers on the same transport add the tracks. Sections an SFU left inactive come after active ones.
    • ICE lite: an SFU that is an ICE lite agent makes this side control ICE, also when it answers.
    • Streams: Vp8EncodedFrame.Ssrc tells the SFU's streams apart.

    Loss, bitrate and orientation

    • Lost packets: the last 512 video packets sent are kept, and sent again, as sent, when the peer asks with a generic NACK, RFC 4585 6.2.1. A gap in the received video is asked for at once, before its frames wait for a keyframe.
    • Bitrate: each receiver report carries a REMB with what the peer may send: the loss-based estimate of BandwidthEstimator, down on heavy loss and slowly up on little, within the cap of RequestBitrateAsync, e.g. when this device cannot decode more. The peer's REMBs, also Chromium's estimates as we announce goog-remb, come in BitrateRequested for the encoder.
    • Sender reports: each second, a sender report of the video and one of the audio sent pair the wall-clock time with each stream's RTP time, so the peer can line picture and sound up, and carry the counts sent.
    • Orientation: the 3GPP video orientation header extension, urn:3gpp:video-orientation, is offered and answered; a sent frame's rotation goes on its last packet, and a received frame's comes with it in Vp8EncodedFrame.Rotation. LiveKit does not answer it, so over LiveKit the rotation does not travel; SendsOrientation tells, and the sender turns its pictures upright itself, with VideoFrame.Rotated.

    Not supported: IPv6 and TCP candidates, mDNS host names (browsers' hidden host candidates; the peer-reflexive path covers them), renegotiation other than ICE restarts, lining up received video with audio, RTX retransmission streams, and transport-wide congestion control.

    Tests

    Uniform.Matrix.WebRtc.Tests checks each layer:

    • STUN against the vectors of RFC 5769, and SRTP against those of RFC 3711 and libsrtp;
    • TURN against an in-process fake server, and against a real one when UNIFORM_MATRIX_TURN (host:port) and UNIFORM_MATRIX_TURN_SECRET (the shared secret) are set;
    • ICE between two agents directly, with a role conflict, and through two fake TURN servers; DTLS between two sides in memory;
    • a whole call with headless Chromium's WebRTC, driven over the DevTools protocol, when Chromium is installed: each side must decode the other's tone.
